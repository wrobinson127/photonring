How it works

1. Light

One ray per pixel, traced backward through the space around a spinning black hole.

g = η + f l l
f = rs r³ / (r⁴ + a²y²)
H = ½ gμν pμ pν

Up to 320 Runge-Kutta steps per ray. These coordinates stay finite at the horizon.

2. Disk

Gas orbits from 1.16 rs outward at spin 0.9 (3 rs at spin 0). Color is a black body at the shifted temperature. Brightness scales as g⁴.

g = (p · u)camera / (p · u)gas

Temperature profile: Page and Thorne (1974).

3. The picture

The arc over the shadow is the far side of the disk. The thin rings are light that circled the hole. The flat edge is the side spinning toward you.

5. Objects

They free-fall by the same equations. You see each where it was when its light left, so one falling in fades just outside the horizon.

6. Drag and tides

The disk drags objects inward. A planet or star breaks up at about

(3 Mhole / 4πρ)1/3

For a hole of 4.3 million Suns: 5.7 rs at Earth's density, 8.9 rs at the Sun's.

7. The ride

R follows the last object launched, through the horizon. Light ahead shifts blue, light behind red.

At spin 0, a camera falling from rest should see the shadow's edge 42.1° from its middle at the horizon. The render agrees within about 0.5%.

8. Nebulae

Twelve Hubble images at their real positions and orientations.

Not physics

Sky. Stars to magnitude 6.5 are real, as seen from Earth. Fainter stars, clouds and galaxies are invented.

Nebulae. Drawn 28 to 604 times too large, as clouds of points. Their depth, the haze around them and the small stars among them are invented.

Disk. No thickness. Streaks are noise.

Objects. Far too large. Drag far too strong.

Debris. Fades by a hand-set rule.

Light delay. First-order for objects.

Ride. Playback slows as you fall. Exposure adapts.

Brightness and exposure are set by eye throughout.
